Grocery price comparison in Australia

Coles and Woolworths publish their prices online. They rarely discount the same products in the same week - so on any given week, part of your regular shop is cheaper at one chain than the other. Grocery price comparison is simply the practice of finding that gap before you shop instead of after.

Why ALDI and IGA prices are hard to compare

This is the single most important thing to understand about the table above, and it is why store count is a poor way to choose.

Coles and Woolworths publish their prices online. Both run full e-commerce catalogues, which means a current, complete, machine-readable price for essentially every product they sell. A comparison between those two can be exact.

ALDI does not. It has no comparable online price list, so any tool showing you an ALDI price has obtained it another way - in-store checks, crowd-sourced submissions, or reading the weekly catalogue. That can still be genuinely useful, particularly for staples that rarely move. But it is a different kind of number, and the fair question to ask any app is how recent is this, and where did it come from?

IGA is a different problem again. IGA stores are independently owned, and pricing is set per store. There is no single "IGA price" to compare against - the shop in one suburb genuinely charges differently from the shop in the next. An app can show you an indicative price, but it cannot promise that is what your local charges.

The practical upshot: a comparison across five chains is not necessarily five times more useful than a comparison across two. It is more useful for the chains with published data and progressively more approximate for the rest. Decide which you need before you pick a tool.

How to compare grocery prices properly

Comparing a headline price is the easy part and the least valuable. These are the things that actually change what you pay.

  1. Compare per unit, not per pack

    The bigger box is not reliably cheaper. Compare price per 100g, per litre or per unit - pack sizes differ between chains specifically often enough that the per-pack price misleads. Both supermarkets print unit pricing by law; the work is in doing the arithmetic consistently.

  2. Check on Wednesday

    Both chains run a Wednesday catalogue cycle. Specials, including half-price promotions, change over then. Comparing on a Monday tells you about a week that is nearly finished.

  3. Know whether a "special" is actually cheap

    A price is only a discount relative to what the product normally costs. Products on a predictable promotional cycle will be half price again soon, which matters when deciding whether to stock up now or wait. Price history is what turns a sticker into a decision.

  4. Compare the basket, not the item

    Almost nobody shops for one product. The chain that wins on your milk may lose on your coffee. What matters is the total for the specific set of things you buy - and, if you are willing to split a shop, which items to buy where.

  5. Watch the things you buy repeatedly

    A one-off saving is a rounding error. The compounding saving comes from the twenty or so items you buy most weeks. Track those, and let something tell you when one of them drops rather than checking manually.

Common questions

What is the best app to compare Coles and Woolworths prices?

It depends on what you want. If you want the widest supermarket coverage, apps that also include ALDI or IGA cover more chains. If you want depth on the two chains that publish their prices - price history, per-unit maths, drop alerts and sending your list to the cheaper trolley - StockUp is built specifically for Coles and Woolworths. Most of these apps are free to try, so the practical answer is to install two and keep whichever matches how you shop.

Is there a free grocery price comparison app in Australia?

Yes, several. StockUp compares Coles and Woolworths prices, shows half-price specials, sends price-drop alerts and builds a Cart split by cheapest store, all free and with no sign-up required. Bargeroo, WiseList, GroceryWise, TrolleyChecker and WhichGrocer also offer free comparison. Some apps put price history or advanced features behind a subscription.

Can you compare ALDI prices in Australia?

Not reliably from published data. ALDI does not publish a full online price list the way Coles and Woolworths do, so any app showing ALDI prices is collecting them another way - in-store checks, crowd-sourcing, or catalogue scanning. That can still be useful, but it is worth knowing how recent the data is before trusting a specific price.

Do supermarket prices vary between states in Australia?

Yes. Coles and Woolworths both run state-based pricing and product ranges, so a product stocked in Victoria may be unavailable or differently priced in Western Australia. Some products are exclusive to particular states entirely. Any comparison is most accurate when it reflects the range for where you actually shop.

How often do supermarket specials change in Australia?

Weekly. Both Coles and Woolworths run a Wednesday catalogue cycle, so half-price specials and most promotional pricing change over on Wednesdays. That makes Tuesday night and Wednesday morning the natural time to check prices and plan a shop.

Are grocery price comparison apps affiliated with the supermarkets?

Generally no. StockUp is independent and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Coles or Woolworths. It uses publicly available pricing data. Independence is the point - a comparison is only useful if it is not run by one of the parties being compared.

How much can you actually save comparing grocery prices?

It varies by basket and week, so treat any fixed figure with suspicion. What is consistent is that the two chains rarely discount the same products in the same week, so on any given week a portion of your regular items is cheaper at one chain than the other. The saving comes from catching that gap on the items you buy repeatedly, rather than from one big switch.
